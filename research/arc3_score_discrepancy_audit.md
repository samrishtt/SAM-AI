## 1. Executive Summary

This forensic audit investigates the severe discrepancy between our official ARC-AGI-3 competition submission (which scored **9.50** on the public leaderboard on October 10, 2026) and our recent B2 local validation evaluation (which reported a headline score of **70.89**).

### Primary Findings:
1. **The 70.89 Score is a Project-Specific Best-of-Four Metric on 10 Known Demo Games, Not an Official RHAE Score:**  
   The reported 70.89 was generated by taking the **maximum score across 4 stochastic passes** on **10 known public demonstration games** (`ar25`, `ft09`, `lp85`, `r11l`, `re86`, `sb26`, `sc25`, `tr87`, `tu93`, `vc33`). It is not an official competition score, and cannot be directly compared to competition submissions.
2. **The Upstream Baseline Comparison Was Apples-to-Oranges:**  
   The upstream Daniel Franzen reference score of **43.36** was obtained from a **single pass ($N=1$)**, whereas B2 reported a **best-of-four ($N=4$) maximum of 70.89**. On a true like-for-like basis (Pass 0 vs. Pass 0), B2 scored **46.07** vs. Franzen's **43.36** (+2.71 points, not +27.53 points).
3. **The Concurrency Trap Explains the 9.50 Competition Collapse:**  
   In B2 Experiment 1, client concurrency was clamped to `bm.solver.concurrency = 10`. While optimal for a 10-game offline suite (1 stream per game), in a 110-game competition run, a concurrency of 10 forces 110 games into sequential batches. Under Kaggle's 9-hour hard timeout, only ~30–35 games could complete before the container was killed. In ARC-AGI-3, unplayed games receive a score of 0.00, which mathematically forces a ~29-point solver down to **~9.50**.
4. **B2 Does Not Evaluate SAM-AI's Sovereign Reasoning Core:**  
   The executed inference path runs Daniel Franzen's Milestone 2 stack: Intel Qwen 3.8B W4A16 AutoRound, Albucino MTP drafter, SGLang Pennyroyal, and Tufa Duck Harness. SAM-AI's 14B model, v4 LoRA adapter, substrate engine, and adaptive controller are **not loaded or executed**. B2 is strictly an **infrastructure reproduction of a third-party solver**, not evidence of SAM-AI's cognitive capability.

## 3. Audit of the Local Scorer (`scripts/evaluate_b2_run.py`)

### 3.1. Game Selection & Scope
* The script evaluated exactly **10 demonstration games**:
  `demo_excluded_games = ['bp35', 'cd82', 'cn04', 'dc22', 'g50t', 'ka59', 'lf52', 'ls20', 'm0r0', 's5i5', 'sk48', 'sp80', 'su15', 'tn36', 'wa30']`
  These 15 games were excluded by Cell 16 when `TRUE_SUBMISSION = False`.
* Out of the 10 included games, **4 are completely won by the baseline** (`ar25`, `ft09`, `sb26`, `tr87`), providing a baseline floor of $400 / 10 = 40.0$ points.
* In the official competition, all **110 hidden games** are evaluated. Generalization on unseen games is drastically lower than on the 10 known demonstration puzzles.

### 3.2. Aggregation & The Best-of-Four Inflation
* `evaluate_b2_run.py` computed the primary score as:
  ```python
  best = max(scores)  # maximum across 4 passes
  official_score = sum(best_scores) / n_games
  ```
* Taking $\max(P_0, P_1, P_2, P_3)$ exploits stochastic sampling variance:
  * In `tr87`: Pass 0 = 4.76, Pass 1 = 71.43, Pass 2 = 70.47, Pass 3 = **100.00** &rarr; Scored as **100.00**.
  * In `ar25`: Pass 0 = 58.33, Pass 1 = 58.33, Pass 2 = **100.00**, Pass 3 = 58.33 &rarr; Scored as **100.00**.
  * In `tu93`: Pass 0 = 20.71, Pass 1 = 33.33, Pass 2 = **62.22**, Pass 3 = 33.33 &rarr; Scored as **62.22**.
* In Kaggle's live competition evaluation, each submission receives **exactly one pass ($N=1$)**. Taking the best of four passes produces a mathematically invalid comparison against single-pass competition runs.

### 3.3. Independent Recalculation from Raw Artifacts
The reported metrics were independently recalculated directly from `benchmark.json` (40 game runs, 1.7 MB):
* **Best-of-Four Mean:** Exactly **70.89**.
* **Diagnostic 40-Run Mean:** Exactly **57.00**.
* **Pass 0 Mean:** Exactly **46.07**.
* **Pass 1 Mean:** Exactly **55.79**.
* **Pass 2 Mean:** Exactly **60.36**.
* **Pass 3 Mean:** Exactly **64.48**.

### 3.4. Scoring Formula Verification
The individual level scores in `benchmark.json` obey the official ARC-AGI-3 RHAE formula:
$$S_g = \frac{\sum_{l=1}^{N_g} l \cdot \min\left(1.15, \left(\frac{H_l}{A_l}\right)^2\right) \cdot C_l}{\frac{N_g(N_g + 1)}{2}}$$
When an agent clears levels with human-parity efficiency ($A_l \le H_l$), the score matches the exact triangular ratio $\frac{k(k+1)}{N_g(N_g+1)}$.

## 4. Audit of Scientific Validity & Causality

### 4.1. Did Experiment 1 Genuinely Isolate Concurrency?
* **Configured Change:** In Cell 16, `bm.solver.concurrency` was set to `10`, matching SGLang's `MAXREQ = 10`. Request timeout was set to `180.0s`.
* **Observed Facts:**
  * In `predictions/franzen_10_output` (legacy concurrency): **16 HTTP read timeouts**, **16 connection pool errors**.
  * In `evidence/b2_exp1_output` (concurrency = 10): **0 HTTP read timeouts**, **0 connection pool errors**.
* **Causal Attribution Retraction:**
  * While the reduction of HTTP timeouts to 0 is an observed fact, the previous claim that *"concurrency was the sole cause of earlier failures"* is an **unsupported causal overstatement**.
  * Other potential factors include request timeout buffer adjustments (180s), SGLang memory fragmentation, mount path stability (`_resolve_dir`), and stochastic rollout noise.

### 4.2. The Concurrency Scaling Trap (10 Games vs. 110 Games)
* In Franzen's original code:
  `bm.solver.concurrency = 120 if TRUE_SUBMISSION else 12`
* **Why Franzen used 120 in submission mode:** There are 110 games in competition. Setting concurrency to 120 launches all 110 games in parallel into the async event loop, advancing all games concurrently in round-robin fashion through SGLang.
* **What happens when concurrency is clamped to 10 in submission mode:**
  * 10 games run concurrently; 100 games wait in the sequential queue.
  * If the first 10 games consume 35–45 minutes each, the 9-hour hard timeout terminates the container after only ~30–35 games are played.
  * In ARC-AGI-3, unplayed games receive a score of **0.00**.
  * If 35 games average ~30.0 points and 75 games score 0.00:
    $$\text{Global Score} = \frac{35 \times 30.0 + 75 \times 0.0}{110} \approx 9.55 \approx \mathbf{9.50}$$
  * This mathematical mechanism completely explains the reported 9.50 competition score.
